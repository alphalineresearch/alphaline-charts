Crypto Market Breadth

Does the share of coins in uptrends tell us anything about where BTC and ETH go next? Tested on weekly top-100 snapshots from January 2016 to October 2026, including every coin that later died.

  • No edgeBreadth does not time BTC or ETH. Strong breadth was followed by better BTC returns in 2016–20, but the link disappeared in 2021–26. Correlation with the next 13 weeks of BTC fell from +0.33 to +0.01.

BTC was higher a year later every time, by +31% to +254%, at every threshold nearby, though with drawdowns of up to 53% along the way and only three cycles behind it. Short-term breadth is high but rolling over; long-term breadth is still near bear-market levels. That combination has preceded a higher BTC a year later in all four past cases, though with only four cases it is a pattern, not a tested signal. BTC is well below its high, so the divergence pattern is not active. High 30-week breadth has also meant a headwind for alts against BTC.

1. Breadth and the next move in BTC

If breadth predicted BTC, strong readings would be followed by better returns in both halves of the sample. They were in 2016–20, when strong breadth and strong BTC went together through two big bull runs. In 2021–26 the pattern is flat to inverted: the 60–80% bucket was followed by slightly negative BTC returns.

A relationship that only shows up in one regime is not something to trade on. Breadth also adds little once you know whether BTC itself is above its own 30-week average.

2. Short and long averages

The same measure with a longer average answers a different question. The share of coins above their 30- or 50-week average tracks the current trend. The share above their 100- or 200-week average shows how far through the cycle the market is.

A coin needs nearly four years of history to be measured against a 200-week average, so that series starts in late 2020 with 15–55 coins and leans toward survivors. The 100-week series starts in 2016 with 40–80 coins.

Short averages lean with the trend over the next quarter, but only in the first half of the sample. Long averages lean against it over the next year: when most coins are above their 100-week average the following year has been weak, and when few are it has been strong. For the 100-week version that held in both halves. The table gives Spearman rank correlations with the BTC or ETH return over the next 13 or 52 weeks; each window's sample is split at its midpoint for the halves.

Two checks temper this. First, 100-week breadth carries no information that BTC's own distance from its 100-week average doesn't already give. Second, entering when long-term breadth first hits a low has been early: lows lasted 71 weeks in 2018–20 and 77 weeks in 2022–23, and BTC was 38% and 48% lower six months after each began. As a gauge of where the cycle stands it is useful; as an entry trigger it is not.

Signal: short-term strong, long-term weak

The signal is on when 60% or more of the top 100 are above their 30-week average while 25% or fewer are above their 100-week average. Weeks on either side of a gap of up to two weeks count as one episode.

Each past episode was an early recovery out of a bear market, and BTC was higher a year after every one. None was a clean ride. BTC fell 53% from its peak within a year of the 2019 episodes, and after February 2020 it was at one point 42% below the signal-week price. These six episodes come from three bear-to-bull turns (2019, 2020 and 2023), so the sample is really three cycles.

After the signal ends

Like the Opportunity Signal, each week can be labelled by how long ago the last episode ended. The weeks after an episode ended were as good as the episode itself or better, so a signal that switched off a few weeks ago is still relevant.

While the signal is on, the next six months have been a coin flip (median −1%, 48% of weeks higher). The second 2019 episode ran into the summer 2019 top. The edge shows at the one-year horizon and in the months after an episode ends.

Every combination of 30-week cut-offs from 50% to 70% and 100-week cut-offs from 15% to 35% gave the same answer: BTC was higher a year after the start of every episode. Each cell shows episodes with BTC higher a year later out of episodes with a full year of data, and the median one-year return.

3. What breadth says about alts against BTC

Most alt analysis uses TOTAL2, TOTAL3 or OTHERS: the summed market cap of everything except BTC (and ETH, or the top 10). Those totals grow whenever a new coin launches or tokens unlock, so they overstate what anyone holding alts actually earned.

The chart compares TOTAL3/BTC with two baskets you could have held: the top-100 alts by market cap, and the same coins equal-weighted, both rebalanced weekly into that week's top 100.

Holding alts instead of BTC cost about 90% since 2016 either way you weight them, and the typical single alt lost essentially everything relative to BTC. Over that time TOTAL3/BTC rose __T3TOTAL__, or __T3XTOTAL__ without stablecoins.

Within that steady bleed, breadth does carry information. The more alts are above their own averages, the worse the basket has done against BTC over the following quarter. The correlation is −0.22 for the next 13 weeks (90% interval −0.38 to −0.04), and −0.26 and −0.20 in the two halves taken separately.

4. Breadth extremes as signals

Three patterns borrowed from equity breadth work, each counted once per episode, with repeats inside 26 weeks ignored. Returns are for the 13 weeks after the signal week. The baseline row is the median for all weeks since 2016.

Divergence: BTC within 10% of its 52-week high, under 50% of alts above their average, down 15+ points in 8 weeks

The exception was November 2016, at the start of the 2017 run. The other five came near local tops: August 2019, May 2023, June 2024, February 2025 and October 2025. With six cases this is a pattern to watch, not a rule.

Washout: 10% or fewer of the top 100 above their 30-week average

March 2020 and September 2024 were superb entries. April and October 2018 and both 2022 washouts were followed by more losses. A washout tells you the market is oversold, not that the selling is over.

Thrust: from 20% or less to 60% or more above the average within 6 weeks

Thrusts are the classic equity breadth buy signal. BTC's median 13-week return after one was +20%, against +10% for all weeks, but 3 of the 7 were negative, and 26 weeks out the median (+23%) was in line with an ordinary week (+26%). The alt basket still lost to BTC after six of seven thrusts.

What I'd put on the site

  • Alts vs. BTC. The holdable alt basket against TOTAL3, both relative to BTC. It is the strongest result here and the one most readers will not have seen.
  • Crypto breadth. Share of the top 100 above their 30-week and 100- or 200-week averages, under BTC, with the divergence and short-strong/long-weak conditions flagged when they are live. Presented as a read on market health and cycle position, not as a BTC timing signal.
  • Skip washout and thrust signals, and any TOTAL2/TOTAL3 chart that implies those totals are returns.

The snapshots come from CoinMarketCap's public website feed, which is free but unofficial and weekly. A site chart could run on it with a fallback, or on a paid source if it ever breaks.

Method

  • Universe. Each week's top 100 by market cap from that week's snapshot, so coins that later collapsed count while they were large. Stablecoins, wrapped and liquid-staked copies (found by price tracking $1, BTC, ETH, SOL or BNB) and tokenized stocks and commodities are removed.
  • Measures. Price above its 30-week average (needs 26 of 30 weeks of history); price above a year ago; 52-week highs and lows; dominance and totals from the top-500 market caps.
  • Testing. Forward returns at 4, 13, 26 and 52 weeks; rank correlations with a block bootstrap, because overlapping weekly windows overstate significance; every result split into 2016–20 and 2021–26. A result counts only if it has the same sign in both halves.
  • Limits. Weekly data, about four crypto cycles, and roughly 20 independent six-month periods. Event studies have six or seven cases each.
