What these securities are

STRK (8%), STRF (10%) and STRD (10%) are Strategy's fixed-rate perpetual preferreds with a $100 face value. When their prices fall, their yields rise. STRK can convert into MSTR shares; STRF is senior and its dividends accumulate if skipped; STRD is the most junior, and its dividends do not accumulate. That makes STRD the most sensitive to doubts about Strategy.

STRC is Strategy's variable-rate preferred. Strategy adjusts its rate to keep the price near $100: it has stepped up from 9.6% at launch to 12% now, paid monthly and then twice a month from mid-2026. BMNP (BitMine) pays $0.185 a week, about $9.60 a year, and SATA (Strive) pays daily, about 13%. Both are too new to test.

1. Who breaks first

For each selloff, the first day each security closed 3% below its 60-day high, and the day of its low. BTC, ETH, MSTR, STRK and STRD started falling within a few days of each other. STRC held near par until late in each selloff, then fell to its low within days of BTC's.

2. STRC as a buy

STRC has a built-in pull back to par: when it trades at a discount, Strategy raises the rate until buyers return. So far that has worked every time, including June 2026, when STRC fell to $74.60 before recovering to $99 within three months.

For BTC, STRC under $97 has been a coin flip: after two of the four (February and June 2026) BTC was higher 60 days later, after the other two it fell further. The pull to par depends on Strategy being willing and able to pay a higher rate. A deeper or longer BTC decline would test that.

3. STRD as an early warning

The fixed-rate preferreds trade every day on Strategy's credit. Their yield over the 10-year Treasury rose from about 7% to over 15% for STRD at the June 2026 low, and has since fallen back to about 9%. When STRD falls below its 50-day average, BTC has usually followed.

BTC's own 50-day break did nearly as well over the same period, so the question is whether STRD breaks earlier. It usually did: in seven of eight cases BTC broke its 50-day average on the same day as STRD or up to 29 days later. Three STRD breaks came while BTC was still above its 50-day average, all in April–May 2026, before the June crash. The latest STRD break, on __BREAK__, is the same setup.

This is fifteen months of data in which BTC mostly fell, from about $120k to $58k and back to $84k. In that kind of market any trend signal looks good. Treat the STRD break as a reason for caution, not a tested sell signal.

A recovery to $98 after trading under $95 looked like relief each time, but it has not marked the end of BTC's decline.

What I'd build

  • Treasury credit monitor. STRD's spread over Treasuries with its 50-day average, and STRC against par, under BTC. Two flags: caution when STRD breaks its 50-day average while BTC is still above its own, and late-stage when STRC closes under $97.
  • Add BMNP and SATA once they have a year of history. BMNP launched in June 2026 near $81 and has traded near par since September.
  • Revisit the numbers after the next selloff. Three episodes is enough to describe what happened, not to rely on.

Method

  • Data. Yahoo Finance daily closes and dividends on US trading days; BTC and ETH sampled on the same days. Total returns reinvest dividends on the ex-date.
  • Events. STRC under $97 counts again only after STRC has been back above $99. Recoveries need a close under $95 first. Breaks are the first close below the 50-day average after a close above it.
  • Yields. Fixed-rate yields use the coupon over the price. STRC's uses its current annualised rate. The spread subtracts the 10-year Treasury yield.
  • Limits. STRK has traded since February 2025, STRC since July 2025, SATA since November 2025 and BMNP since June 2026. Every result here rests on three selloffs.
